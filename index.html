I study how microbes and the environment write themselves into the human genome, and what those mutational footprints tell us about why colorectal cancer is rising in young adults.

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in Ludmil Alexandrov's lab at UC San Diego. My work sits where mutational-signature analysis meets the cancer microbiome. In the international Mutographs colorectal cancer study, I led the microbiome analyses linking pks+ bacteria to the colibactin signature (SBS88). That signature is enriched in early-onset disease, but bacterial presence alone does not predict mutagenic activity. Host context and the timing of exposure matter.

I also build open tools. HRProfiler detects homologous recombination deficiency from whole-genome and whole-exome data. pksProfiler and a scalable Nextflow cancer-microbiome pipeline detect genotoxic bacteria in tumour and metagenome sequencing.

I earned my PhD in Bioinformatics & Systems Biology at UC San Diego (2025) and a BA in Computer Science and Biology at Mount Holyoke College (2019). Before graduate school I worked at the Massachusetts General Hospital Cancer Center with Aaron Hata and Mike Lawrence, studying APOBEC mutagenesis in drug-tolerant lung cancer cells.

Research

Four threads, one question: which processes damage our DNA, and how can we read them from a tumour?

Microbes & mutagenesis

Colibactin, pks+ bacteria and early-onset colorectal cancer

I led microbiome-integrated analyses across a large international colorectal cancer cohort to link microbial exposures to somatic mutational signatures. The colibactin signature SBS88 is enriched in early-onset disease. Bacterial presence alone does not predict mutagenic activity, which points to host state and the timing of exposure as the missing variables.

Díaz-Gay et al., Nature 2025 · Abbasi & Alexandrov, Cancer Cell 2026
Precision oncology

Detecting homologous recombination deficiency

HRProfiler is a machine-learning classifier that uses just six mutational features to detect HRD, a biomarker for PARP-inhibitor and platinum sensitivity. It works on whole-exome data, where other tools fail. With DeepHRD, the same biomarker is predicted directly from routine H&E slides.

Abbasi et al., Cancer Res 2025 · Bergstrom et al., J Clin Oncol 2024
Methods

Scalable mutational-signature discovery

I contribute to the SigProfiler suite of tools for de novo signature extraction and for exploring large mutational events across thousands of genomes. I have also written about what sequencing technologies can and cannot resolve.

Islam et al., Cell Genomics 2022 · Khandekar et al., BMC Genomics 2023 · Abbasi & Alexandrov, DNA Repair 2021
Cancer evolution

Mutational processes that shape evolution and resistance

Mutational processes are dynamic and clinically relevant, not static background. Examples include therapy-induced APOBEC3A in persistent lung cancer cells, viral EBNA1 breaking chromosomal fragile sites, copy-number signatures across human cancer and tobacco-driven mutagenesis.

Isozaki et al., Nature 2023 · Li et al., Nature 2023 · Steele et al., Nature 2022

Software

Research that ships as tools other people can run.

HRProfiler
Python · PyPI · first-author tool

Predicts homologous recombination deficiency from whole-genome or whole-exome somatic mutations using six interpretable features.

GitHub →PyPI →Paper →
pksProfiler
Nextflow · conda

Detects and quantifies the colibactin-producing pks island in tumour genomes and metagenomes, and identifies which bacterium is carrying it.

GitHub →
CMPipeline
Nextflow · HPC

Cancer-microbiome profiling of whole genomes. It removes host reads, then keeps only the taxa that both KrakenUniq/Bracken and MetaPhlAn 4 agree on. Optional steps confirm species by alignment and remove reagent contaminants.

GitHub →
SigProfiler suite
Contributor · Alexandrov Lab

Community-standard tools for building mutational matrices and extracting signatures. I contributed to SigProfilerExtractor and SigProfilerMatrixGenerator.

Cancer repeat profiling

Maps the landscape of the EBV-like repeat at chromosome 11q23 across populations using long-read sequencing. This is the repeat EBNA1 breaks.
